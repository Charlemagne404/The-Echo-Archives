# Echo Archives writing guide

This guide covers first-party interface copy and the editorial framing around the archive. It does not replace imported show descriptions, creator-supplied material, reviews, listener reviews, factual metadata, or legal text.

## Tone

Write like a small, attentive audio-drama archive: specific, clear, understated, confident, and slightly warm. A little atmosphere is welcome when it helps someone choose what to listen to. Prefer concrete details—mood, tone, format, pace, runtime, completion status, and the action a page supports—over broad claims about quality or impact.

## Prefer

- Say what a page or control does: “Browse shows”, “Send a correction”, and “Read the review”.
- Describe listening situations and useful distinctions rather than promising a better “experience”.
- Give collections a reason to exist: a shared mood, format, commitment, or connection to a show.
- Vary headings and sentence shapes. Short is good, but every section does not need the same rhythm.
- Keep Archive Rating, Listener Review Score, Community Rating, and Creator Verified visibly and verbally separate.

## Avoid

- Generic slogans and broad promises such as “discover what matters”, “unlock”, “seamless”, or “powerful discovery”.
- Repeated copy patterns such as “Whether you…”, “Built to…”, “Designed to…”, “More than…”, and “X without the Y”.
- Vague praise words when a concrete description will do: “meaningful”, “comprehensive”, “effortless”, “immersive”, or “unique”.
- Explaining a heading twice, stacking symmetrical benefit lists, or making ordinary controls sound grand.
- Claims about human selection, creator approval, or editorial involvement unless the repository and workflow support them.

## By surface

- Headings should orient the reader or express a real listening question, not announce a marketing promise.
- Buttons should name the result of the click. Use “Learn more” only when the destination genuinely needs that neutral label.
- Collection copy should explain the common thread or the listener it suits. Do not force every description into “A collection of…”.
- Editorial copy may have taste and texture; interface copy should be shorter and clearer.
- Help copy should be direct and practical. Empty and error states can be human, but do not turn every dead end into a joke.

## Exceptions

Preserve factual metadata, imported or attributed descriptions, reviews, listener submissions, quotations, accessibility meaning, and legal obligations. Clarify legal or support copy only when the underlying behavior and meaning stay the same.
