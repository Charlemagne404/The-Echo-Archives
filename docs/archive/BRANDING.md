# Branding Guidance

## Core naming

- The product name is **The Echo Archives**
- The parent or studio brand is **Continental**

User-facing branding should prioritize **The Echo Archives**.

## Recommended relationship

Good footer language:

- "A Continental project"
- "A product of Continental Studios"

Avoid:

- renaming the product to "Continental Echo Archives"
- making Continental louder than the archive itself on primary product screens

Continental should feel like the umbrella. The Echo Archives should feel like the thing people actually use.

## Product personality

The site should feel:

- cinematic
- archival
- dark
- curated
- human
- discovery-focused

It should not sound like:

- a corporate media brand
- a venture-backed product deck
- a generic SEO content farm

## Visual direction

The current visual direction is good and should not be thrown away.

Keep the strengths:

- dark cinematic tone
- confident hero
- atmospheric imagery
- strong product identity

The revamp should improve structure and scalability, not flatten the brand into a generic catalog UI.

## Voice

Write like someone who genuinely likes audio dramas and wants to help other people find good ones.

Good traits:

- clear
- specific
- opinionated when useful
- welcoming without sounding fake

Bad traits:

- corporate
- overexplained
- algorithm worship
- fake-neutral database language

## Assistant naming

Preferred assistant framing:

- **Ask the Archivist**

Less strong:

- Open chat
- AI assistant

The assistant should feel like a feature of the archive, not a separate product bolted on top.

## Collections and taxonomy tone

Collections should feel thematic but understandable.

Good examples:

- Best for long walks
- Cold isolation horror
- Funny space disasters
- Serious sci-fi

Avoid labels that are so clever they stop being useful.

## Parent-brand balance

The Echo Archives should be the front-facing brand because it has the clearest audience and use case. Continental benefits more from owning a strong product than from swallowing the product identity whole.
