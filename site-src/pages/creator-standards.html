Creator standards

Echo Archives is built to help listeners trust what they are seeing. These standards explain how we handle creator submissions, factual updates, verification, and editorial boundaries.

What guides archive decisions

Accuracy first

Facts, links, credits, and release details need a trustworthy source trail.

Transparency

Verification confirms factual metadata only. It does not imply approval of ratings, reviews, or curation.

Respectful participation

Submission channels exist to improve the archive, not to harass, spam, or distort it.

Clarity for listeners

Metadata should set honest expectations for tone, format, status, and listening commitment.

Rights and originality

Archive records should respect ownership, attribution, and the distinction between official and editorial material.

Ongoing review

Archive entries can be updated over time, but every public change still goes through review.

Verification and trust

What verification changes

  • It adds a factual trust signal to a show record when the archive has official or representative proof.
  • It can improve credits, links, release details, and other objective metadata.
  • It helps separate confirmed facts from placeholders or unresolved fields.
Editorial independence

What verification does not change

  • Archive Ratings remain editorial and are never bought, negotiated, or creator-approved.
  • Listener reviews stay moderated community content, separate from creator input.
  • Collection placement, archive notes, and spotlight decisions remain editorial choices.
